Transponder mount and cage

UBC Baja chassis team, designed October 2025

A rule-compliant mount that holds the team’s TR2 MX timing transponder vertically on the frame and shields it from debris without interfering with its signal.

Design decisions

  • Frame mount instead of body panel: the transponder must be installed vertically on the frame, on the right side forward of the seat, no more than 61 cm (24 in) above the ground (B.13.2.3.1). Mounting to a body panel was compared with welding a tab to the frame, and the frame gives a more consistently vertical orientation.
  • Steel tab for structure: a 0.063 in steel tab welded to the frame carries the transponder, following the rules’ suggestion to weld a small plate to the frame for its bracket (B.13.2.3.3).
  • ABS cage for protection: metal and carbon fibre can interrupt the transponder’s signal (B.13.2.3.4), so the housing is 3D-printed ABS.
  • Protection level: only the bottom must stay open so the antenna has a clear path to the ground (B.13.2.3.1). Because debris impact is the main load on this part, the cage encloses every other side based on the likely impact directions, which also stiffens it.
  • Service access: the cage is two pieces. A fixed back piece stays on the mount, and the main cage slides on and off it, held by two cotter pins (5/8 in eye diameter), so the transponder can be reached quickly.
  • Fasteners: flat-head bolts keep the bolt heads clear of the transponder. The stack, in order, is the transponder’s own bracket, the steel tab, and the cage back piece.

Verification

A jig and a level confirmed the tab was vertical during fabrication. The car passed technical inspection with the mount fitted.

Parts: 0.063 in steel sheet, ABS filament, cotter pins, flat-head bolts, and the bracket supplied with the transponder.
