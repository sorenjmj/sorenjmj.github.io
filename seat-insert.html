Driver seat lumbar insert

UBC Baja chassis team, 2025–26

A custom 3D-printed insert that gives Baja drivers lumbar support in a seat that left the lower back unsupported, developed and validated through testing with the team’s drivers.

Problem

The seat did not support the lumbar region. In the car’s awkward seated position, drivers’ rounded backs hurt, which is a comfort issue and also a performance issue over a long race.

Requirements

  • Support the lumbar region without changing the existing seat.
  • Stay within the Baja SAE limit on seat foam padding thickness (2.0 in maximum, B.4.5.1.1), so the fix could not simply be thicker foam.
  • Fit the seat’s actual geometry.
  • Be cheap and fast to iterate, since the team’s testing budget was small.

Design decisions

  • Insert under the foam: because the rules limit foam thickness, the support is a separate insert that sits beneath the existing foam instead of extra padding on top.
  • Shape from the seat CAD: the insert profile was fitted to the seat model, then built out around the lumbar area. It is shallower in the center and thicker toward the edges.
  • 3D printing in PETG: printing made it practical to test several physical versions quickly and at low cost.

Testing with drivers

Research into insert options and usable materials came first. Prototype inserts were then printed and tried by drivers in the seat. With limited budget for instrumented testing, driver feedback was the main evaluation method, since discomfort and support are felt by the person in the seat and are hard to capture any other way. Feedback from each round guided the shape of the next.

Result

Drivers reported feeling better supported during races, and the back pain in the awkward seated position was resolved. The car passed technical inspection and competed with the insert.
